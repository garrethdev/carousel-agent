Strategic read

In under-eye care, the winning play is a question or relatable-scenario opener about dark circles that leads into an 'honest rating' of multiple eye creams — a format that repeatedly turns tiny accounts into millions of views.

Winning patterns

  1. Relatable-scenario hooks are the highest-performing hook type in the niche by a wide margin
    Evidence: relatable_scenario: 9% of all, 22% of top tier (lift 2.59), median 1,449,782 views — vs 346,329 median for the dataset. Named examples: 'me with raccoon under eyes:' (2,428,118 | 59.3x), 'Not breathing until I get rid of my dark circles' (1,449,782), 'Just as when I thought my genetic dark circles were forever..' (100,758 | 2651.5x from a 38-follower account).
    Apply: Open slide 1 with the feeling, not the product: a first-person, slightly self-mocking caption over a raw under-eye close-up ('me with raccoon under eyes:'). Only reveal the eye cream from slide 3 onward, once the viewer has recognised themselves.
  2. Question hooks over-index in the top tier and own the single biggest outlier in the dataset
    Evidence: question: 14% of all, 22% of top tier (lift 1.56), median 691,679 views. Named examples: 'Dark circles?' (17,053,067 | 96.4x — the dataset max), '"Does bad sleep actually give you dark circles?"' (1,374,746), 'Ladies who are in their mid 20s... what under eye cream are we using??' (691,679), 'Which Eye Cream Is For You? WITH FEATURES AND KEY INGREDIENTS' (48,436 | 8x).
    Apply: Write hooks as a direct question the viewer is already asking in the mirror or in their group chat. Two flavours work: the diagnostic question ('Does X actually cause dark circles?') and the crowd-sourcing question ('what under eye cream are we using??') which also farms comments.
  3. Ultra-short, 1-3 word hooks outperform long descriptive hooks
    Evidence: 'Dark circles?' (17,053,067 | 96.4x), 'EYE OPENER' (1,335,942 | 25.6x), 'Dark.' (505,965 | 12.2x) — all three sit in the top 14 hooks, while the long descriptive hook 'Tired eyes Before Dark circles using the Abib Collagen Eye Cream, swipe to see what my under eyes area looks like now!' lands at 1,018,053 and 'The Best Products for Under-Eye Dark Circles' at only 149,076 (1.1x).
    Apply: Cap slide-1 text at 1-4 words in large type over a tight under-eye crop. Push all context (product names, routine detail, timelines) to slides 2-6 where the viewer has already committed to swiping.
  4. The 'honest rating' multi-product review is the format engine of the niche
    Evidence: Named top-40 hooks: 'Honest Rating of Eye Creams' (2,243,066 | 153.7x), 'Rating viral Kbeauty Eye creams For dark circles' (1,440,420 | 98.7x), 'Rating eye creams for fine lines & wrinkles' (992,823 | 68x), 'Honest Eye Cream Review PERFECT FOR ANTI AGING, EYE BAGS AND DARK CIRCLES' (764,500 | 21x), 'Rating Viral Korean Eye Creams' (750,205 | 73.2x), 'Rating Viral Kbeauty Eye creams for dark circles' (552,614 | 37.9x), 'Honest rating of under eye creams' (352,940 | 24.2x).
    Apply: Build carousels as scored line-ups: one product per slide with a visible score, price and a one-line verdict, and put your own product in the line-up honestly (including one flaw). State the concern in the title — 'for dark circles', 'for fine lines' — so the rating has a job.
  5. K-beauty / Korean framing is a reliable multiplier on the rating format
    Evidence: 'Rating viral Kbeauty Eye creams For dark circles' (1,440,420 | 98.7x), 'Rating Viral Korean Eye Creams' (750,205 | 73.2x), 'Rating Viral Kbeauty Eye creams for dark circles' (552,614 | 37.9x), 'Honest Korean skincare eye cream reviews' (141,955 | 1.9x), 'My dark circles before korean skincare 😩👀 Day 1.....' (73,314 | 15.4x), plus 'Tired eyes Before Dark circles using the Abib Collagen Eye Cream...' (1,018,053 | 39.4x).
    Apply: Position your eye cream inside the K-beauty conversation: rate it against the viral Korean eye creams people are already searching for, borrow the ingredient literacy (collagen, peptide, caffeine) and use the word 'viral' in the title.
  6. Format beats follower count — the niche is a proven small-account breakout zone
    Evidence: 9 of 35 slideshows are small-account breakouts: 7 followers -> 51,705 (7386.4x, 'Honest rating of eye cream for dark circles!!! (Got me from this)'), 38 -> 100,758 (2651.5x), 728 -> 309,064 (424.5x), 14,593 -> 2,243,066 (153.7x), 2,688 -> 346,329 (128.8x). Five of the nine breakouts use the rating/honest-rating format.
    Apply: Run the same rating and raccoon-eyes templates from creator seed accounts and UGC partners with tiny followings — the data says the template, not the audience size, carries the reach. No paid-style polish needed.
  7. 'Dark circles' is the dominant search-and-scroll term; puffiness and fine lines are secondary
    Evidence: 'Dark circles' appears in the hook of the #1 (17,053,067), #2 (2,428,118), #4 (1,449,782), #5 (1,440,420), #6 (1,374,746) and #8 (1,018,053) slideshows. The fine-lines variant 'Rating eye creams for fine lines & wrinkles' still hits 992,823 (68x), and the ageing/eye-bag combo 'Honest Eye Cream Review PERFECT FOR ANTI AGING, EYE BAGS AND DARK CIRCLES' hits 764,500 (21x).
    Apply: Lead every eye-care carousel with the phrase 'dark circles' in slide 1 and on-image text, then use slides 4-6 to broaden into puffiness, eye bags and fine lines so one asset serves multiple concerns.
  8. The niche is currently under-supplied — almost nothing has posted recently
    Evidence: Posted last 30d: 0; last 90d: 2, out of 35 slideshows over 25,000 views (median 346,329, p90 1,449,782).
    Apply: Ship a batch now: 3-4 rating carousels plus 2 relatable-scenario carousels in the next two weeks while there is no fresh competition on the format.

Slide structure

  • Slide count: 6 slides (one hook slide + 4 content slides + 1 verdict slide); the rating format naturally supports 6-8 if you score more products
  • Arc: Hook (concern named in 1-4 words) → recognition/relatability or the myth being tested → the scored line-up, one product per slide with score + price + one-line verdict → your pick and why → verdict/recap slide that repeats the concern keyword
  • Text density: Very low on slide 1 (1-4 words, huge type); low-to-medium on slides 2-6 (a score, a product name, one short verdict line per slide) — no paragraphs
  • Visual style: Tight, unretouched under-eye crops in natural light for the hook and recognition slides; flat-lay or hand-held product shots with a big numeric score badge for the rating slides; consistent scoring template across all rating slides so the format is recognisable
  • Notes: Slide-level attributes are missing for this niche: narrative_arc, text_density, content_format, emotional_tone, slide count, face visible, before/after, background, foreground and subject age are all 100% 'unknown' because all 35 items come from the hook corpus (hook_corpus=35, no agent items). That is missing data, not a finding. The structure above is inferred from the hook types and the exact hook wording — e.g. the nine 'rating' hooks imply a one-product-per-slide scored list, and 'swipe to see what my under eyes area looks like now!' (1,018,053) implies a swipe-payoff on a later slide. Also note has CTA: no is 100% of all and 100% of top tier (lift 1) — the corpus simply contains no in-slide CTAs, so keep any CTA soft and in the caption rather than burning a slide on it. Panel-word and save-rate medians are unavailable (n/a), so treat word counts as a design guardrail, not a benchmark.

The final slide names the winner for dark circles and the winner for puffiness so the post ends on a usable verdict rather than a sell.
    Audience: Women 25-38 who have bought three eye creams already and don't believe any of them worked · Tone: Blunt, unglamorous, specific, credible · Hook: Honest rating of eye creams for dark circles
  2. Raccoon eyes club
    A meme-to-fix carousel: it opens on an unretouched front-camera under-eye crop with a group-chat caption, then walks through four shared indignities of tired eyes — creased concealer, 8 hours of sleep and still shadows, 3pm shadows, being asked if you're okay — before showing the caffeine eye serum as the only step that changed the morning routine.
    Audience: Women 22-32 with young kids or shift work who wake up with shadowed, puffy eyes · Tone: Self-deprecating, funny, warm, unfiltered · Hook: me with raccoon under eyes:
  3. Dark circle myth check
    Six slides that put the four usual suspects for dark circles on trial — bad sleep, screen time, genetics and thin under-eye skin — giving each a one-line verdict slide, then closing with which ingredient realistically helps which cause. It reframes the reader's problem so the product recommendation feels like a diagnosis, not an ad.
    Audience: Women 28-45 who sleep well, drink water and still have hereditary-looking circles · Tone: Curious, myth-busting, factual, reassuring · Hook: Does bad sleep actually cause dark circles?

Avoid

  • Don't build the hook as a before/after reveal: before_after is 17% of all slideshows but only 11% of the top tier (lift 0.65) with a median of just 168,439 views versus 346,329 overall. Keep transformation photos as the payoff on slides 4-6, not as the opener.
  • Don't use story-tease openers like '3 reasons why I started taking under eye care seriously from this...' (346,329) or 'castor oil is working for me...' (95,214): story_tease is 6% of all and 0% of the top tier (lift 0, median 220,772).
  • Don't lead with a tutorial promise — tutorial_promise is 3% of all, 0% of top tier (lift 0) and has the worst median in the dataset at 32,231 views. Teach inside the slides, but hook on a question or a scenario.
  • Don't rely on generic superlative bold claims with no rating mechanic or concern named: 'The Best Products for Under-Eye Dark Circles' managed only 149,076 views (1.1x) and bold_claim overall under-indexes in the top tier (51% of all vs 44% of top tier, lift 0.86). Superlatives only work when wrapped in 'honest rating of…'.
  • Don't copy the off-category breakouts that happen to mention dark circles — the lash-serum hooks ('Best and cheapest lash serum that actually works and doesn't cause dark circles', 394,323 and 309,064). They drive traffic that never converts on eye cream and dilute the dark-circle keyword.
  • Don't touch medical or DIY-remedy framing: the hashimotos hook ('I was 27 I had hashimotos Baggy under eyes puffy face Fatigued...') sits at 83,524 and the castor-oil cure claim at 95,214, both well below the 346,329 median — and both are brand-safety risks (implied diagnosis and unproven cure claims).
  • Don't spend a slide on a hard CTA: 100% of the corpus, including 100% of the top tier, carries no in-slide CTA (lift 1) and no CTA types are recorded. Put the ask in the caption or a soft final-slide line instead.
