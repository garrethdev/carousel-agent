Strategic read

In eye-mask carousels the win comes from tiny, cinematic 1-4 word hooks and comparison/scene framing (comparison, relatable_scenario and story_tease all lift 3.43, medians 400,987-630,623 views) — save the before/after and the collagen science for the swipe, not the first panel.

Winning patterns

  1. Ultra-short, 1-4 word hooks that read like a film title, not a caption
    Evidence: Seven of the top-ranked hooks are 1-4 words: "My mask" (630,623 | 198.3x), "mask on" (617,895), "Mask on Mask off" (540,836 | 35.5x), "Real eyes.." (513,347 | 105.8x), "High Collagen" (261,137 | 4080.3x), "eyes" (251,376 | 72.7x), "hey, mask!" (219,916 | 28.9x).
    Apply: Cap slide 1 at four words set large over a close-up of patched under-eyes. Let the image carry the information; put the product name, the claim and the routine on slides 2-6.
  2. Comparison / scene / tease framing beats claim framing by a wide margin
    Evidence: hook_type comparison: 8% of all but 29% of top tier (lift 3.43), median 400,987 views; relatable_scenario lift 3.43, median 630,623; story_tease lift 3.43, median 513,347 — versus bold_claim at lift 0.38, median 42,366. Named examples: "Mask on Mask off" (540,836), "High Collagen" (261,137), "My mask" (630,623), "Real eyes.." (513,347).
    Apply: Open on a state-vs-state device (mask on / mask off, 6am / 6:20am, patch side / bare side) or a lived moment ("My mask"), never on a benefit statement.
  3. Deliver the before/after as the payoff inside the carousel, not as the hook line
    Evidence: before_after hooks are 29% of all and 29% of top tier — lift 0.98, median 71,901 views — while comparison (lift 3.43, median 400,987) and relatable_scenario (lift 3.43, median 630,623) over-index in the top tier. The one huge before/after hook was highly specific: "Tired eyes Before Dark circles using the Abib Collagen Eye Cream, swipe to see what my under eyes area looks like now!" (1,018,053 | 39.4x).
    Apply: Hook with the scene; reveal the split-screen on slide 4 or 5. If you must label a before/after up front, name the product and add an explicit swipe instruction, as the 1,018,053-view hook did.
  4. The word 'collagen' is the niche's curiosity magnet — but only in short, non-listicle form
    Evidence: Short collagen hooks broke out hard: "High Collagen" (261,137 | 4080.3x, 64 followers), "Collagen boosting" (113,465 | 88.1x), "Before and after Collagen" (71,901 | 62.7x), "40 days of collagen" (12,549 | 93.6x). Long claim versions collapsed: bold_claim lift 0.38, median 42,366 ("BEST BENEFITS OF COLLAGEN" 85,773; "5 Benefits of Collagen ☝️" 31,592).
    Apply: Use two-word ingredient stamps ("High collagen", "Collagen boosting") as slide-1 text or as a repeated corner stamp, and explain the mechanism in one sentence on slide 3.
  5. Zero-follower accounts win this niche — the hook does all the distribution work
    Evidence: 8 of 24 slideshows are small-account breakouts (<50k followers, >=50x follower views), including 64 followers -> 261,137 views (4080.3x, "High Collagen"), 1,460 -> 189,499 (129.8x), 4,850 -> 513,347 (105.8x, "Real eyes.."), 1,146 -> 71,901 (62.7x).
    Apply: Ship formats that need no credibility scaffolding: no talking-head intro, no follower proof, no brand-lore slide. First panel = image + <=4 words.
  6. Editorial, no-CTA carousels are the norm — nothing in this corpus sells on-slide
    Evidence: has CTA: no — 100% of all 24 slideshows and 100% of the 7 top-tier slideshows; CTA types list is empty. Median engagement 1.4%.
    Apply: End on the payoff image or a one-line routine note instead of "link in bio"/"comment SHOP". Push conversion to caption and comments so the final slide stays a visual reward.

Slide structure

  • Slide count: 6 slides (brief-mandated); the data cannot confirm an optimal count — slide count is 'unknown' for 100% of the corpus
  • Arc: Scene hook (<=4 words) -> the relatable problem moment -> the mechanism in one line (collagen/hydrogel doing the work) -> proof: split-screen or dated grid -> how it fits a real night/morning routine -> payoff close-up, no CTA
  • Text density: Very low: 2-6 words per panel, one idea per slide, single large sans-serif line. Panel-word medians are n/a in this dataset, so this comes from the hook evidence — the top hooks average 1-4 words and the long claim hooks (bold_claim, lift 0.38, median 42,366) underperformed
  • Visual style: Phone-shot, unretouched close-ups of the under-eye area with the patch clearly visible; two-state pairings (on/off, 6am/6:20am, bare side/patched side); soft bathroom or bedside lighting; ingredient stamp in the corner. Content angles

  1. Mask on / mask off — A six-panel two-state loop: same face, same lighting, alternating patched and bare under-eyes across the morning, so the swipe itself becomes the transformation. Designer note: identical crop and camera distance on every slide so only the eye area changes, with the words 'on' and 'off' alternating in the same corner.
    Emotion: Satisfying visual click — the pleasure of a clean two-state reveal · Hook: Patches on Patches off
  2. Real eyes at 6am — Relatable-scenario diary of one badly-slept morning: creased, puffy, dark under-eyes timestamped 6:02am, patches applied 6:05am, coffee, dog, 6:25am de-puffed. Designer note: timestamp overlays in the top-left of each panel, zero retouching, phone flash allowed.
    Emotion: Recognition and relief — 'that is literally my face' · Hook: Real eyes..
  3. Hydrogel vs silicone — Head-to-head comparison of single-use collagen hydrogel patches versus reusable silicone patches: adhesion, slip, wear time, cost per wear, which one survives a 20-minute walk around the flat. Designer note: split every panel down the middle, left = hydrogel, right = silicone, one verdict word per slide.
    Emotion: Decision confidence — settling a purchase debate in six swipes · Hook: Hydrogel on Silicone on
  4. High collagen, explained — The mechanism angle told in stamps rather than sentences: what collagen and peptides actually do in the thin under-eye skin, why hydrogel drives it in, and why 15 minutes beats 3 minutes. Designer note: ingredient stamp repeated in the same corner of all six panels, one short line of copy per slide, macro shots of the patch texture.
    Emotion: Smart-shopper curiosity — understanding the thing you already own · Hook: High collagen
  5. 40 nights of patches — A time-bound consistency diary: night 1, 5, 12, 25, 33, 40 shot in the same mirror at the same time, with one honest note per date (what changed, what did not). Designer note: date badge burned into every photo, no filters, final panel is the two extremes side by side.
    Emotion: Earned trust — proof that looks like a receipt, not an ad · Hook: 40 nights of eye patches

Carousel briefs

  1. Mask on mask off
    Six identically framed close-ups alternate between bare and patched under-eyes across one morning, so swiping physically toggles the transformation. Slides 3 and 5 add a single word each — 'flat', 'awake' — while the product stays anonymous until the last panel. The whole carousel teaches one thing: 15 minutes of wear visibly changes the eye area.
    Audience: Women 25-38 who apply makeup on tired eyes before work and hate how concealer creases · Tone: Sleek, deadpan, satisfying, confident · Hook: Patches on Patches off
  2. Real eyes, 6am
    A timestamped one-morning diary starting with unretouched 6:02am puffiness and ending at 6:25am after collagen hydrogel patches, coffee and cold water. Each slide is one moment with one short line, and the mechanism gets a single sentence on slide 4. It shows the routine as it actually happens, not as a tutorial.
    Audience: Women 30-45 who wake up with puffy, creased under-eyes after 5 hours of sleep · Tone: Honest, warm, unfiltered, wry · Hook: Real eyes..
  3. Hydrogel vs silicone
    A six-slide split-screen showdown between single-use collagen hydrogel patches and reusable silicone patches, judged on grip, wear time, comfort under makeup, cost per wear and travel. Each panel delivers one verdict word so the reader can stop at any slide and still have an answer. The final panel names which one to buy for which morning.
    Audience: Value-conscious skincare buyers 22-35 deciding between a subscription patch pack and one reusable set · Tone: Direct, practical, comparative, unfussy · Hook: Hydrogel on Silicone on

  • Question hooks. 8% of all slideshows, 0% of the top tier (lift 0), median 187,471 — "WHY IS COLLAGEN IMPORTANT?" (189,499) and "why are eye patches so popular these days?" (185,443) both plateaued below the 261,137-view top-tier threshold.
  • Generic, product-free before/after labels. before_after hooks sit at lift 0.98 (median 71,901) and the worst performer of the whole set was "COLLAGEN BEFORE & AFTER" (47,442, 0.5x). If you label a before/after, name the product and add a swipe instruction like the 1,018,053-view hook did.
  • Chasing the dataset's #1 post. "It Girl" Watches Under $300 (1,197,741 | 2037x) is off-niche accessory bait, not eye-mask content — it inflates the max/p90 and is not replicable or on-brand for a skincare account.
  • On-slide CTAs and sales panels. 0 of 24 slideshows, including 0 of the 7 top-tier posts, used any CTA (CTA types list is empty); keep 'link in bio' and discount codes in the caption so slide 6 stays a visual payoff.
  • Off-niche collagen spillover topics. "Homemade Collagen Powder" (42,366), "COLLAGEN RICH SKIN JUICES" (141,411) and "Collagen Hair Repair before" (10,364) pull ingestible/hair audiences that do not convert to under-eye patches, and ingestible-collagen efficacy claims are a brand-safety risk.
