Strategic read

In anti-aging, visual proof beats claims: before/after (lift 1.75), direct-address (lift 1.58) and relatable-scenario (lift 1.32) hooks built around one hero ingredient — retinol — massively outperform the saturated bold-claim/tutorial posts that make up most of the niche.

Winning patterns

  1. Before/after framing is the single highest-lift hook type in the niche
    Evidence: before_after: 12% of all but 21% of top tier (lift 1.75), median 338,707 views vs 141,411 dataset median. Named winners: 'Me before using retinol:' (4,165,965), 'My skin while using retinol every day' (1,676,221), 'Tired eyes Before Dark circles using the Abib Collagen Eye Cream...' (1,018,053), 'here's my 3 weeks retinol journey (notice how my smile lines are almost gone)' (895,011 | 477.6x).
    Apply: Open slide 1 on the 'before' state only (bare face, unretouched, one named concern like smile lines or crepey under-eyes), then reveal the 'after' mid-carousel so the swipe is the payoff. Always name the timeframe and the exact area that changed.
  2. Second-person 'this is your sign' direct address converts scroll into swipe
    Evidence: direct_address: 7% of all, 11% of top tier (lift 1.58), median 254,903 views. Named winners: 'This is your sign to use retinol 🧖‍♀️✨💖' (892,417 | 51.9x), 'if you were looking for a sign to start using Retinol...' (682,260 | 13.6x), 'Rating Retinols as a medical aesthetician that works in derm' (254,903 | 181.9x).
    Apply: Write the hook to one hesitant person ('you'), not to an audience. Slide 1 = permission/nudge, slides 2-5 = the objections she has (irritation, 'am I too young/too late', purging), slide 6 = the reassuring next step.
  3. Relatable-scenario / cultural-commentary hooks deliver the highest median views of any type
    Evidence: relatable_scenario: 4% of all, 5% of top tier (lift 1.32) but median 614,352 views — the highest median in the lift table. Includes the dataset max: 'young girls getting a 10-step skincare routine, anti aging products...trying to be older' (17,956,543 | 5677.1x, from a 3,163-follower account) and 'just when you want to give up there will be someone who will tell you to use Retinol' (614,352).
    Apply: Lead with an observed skincare behaviour or shared frustration everyone recognises (over-buying actives, the 'just use retinol' advice loop, starting anti-aging too late), then use the carousel to correct it with your ingredient education.
  4. Head-to-head comparison hooks (best vs worst, X vs Y) are breakout machines for tiny accounts
    Evidence: Three named comparison hooks in the top lists: 'Best 🏆 vs Worst 💀 for wrinkles' (1,221,622 | 203.6x from 6,000 followers), 'High Collagen' (261,137 | 4080.3x from 64 followers), 'Retinol vs Retinal Which one should you use?' (80,447 | 1200.7x from 67 followers).
    Apply: Build a 6-slide verdict carousel: slide 1 the versus title, slides 2-5 one criterion per slide (strength, irritation, results timeline, who it's for), slide 6 the winner. Use trophy/skull or ✅/❌ marks so the judgement is readable in half a second.
  5. Explicit results timelines and day-counts are the most shareable information format
    Evidence: 'HOW LONG TO SEE RESULTS? RETINOL 2-4 months NIACINAMIDE 8-12 weeks SALICYLIC ACID 4-6 weeks...' (2,469,669 | 99.3x), 'here's my 3 weeks retinol journey' (895,011 | 477.6x), 'Collagen Cream 52 Yr Old Mom Has Zero Wrinkles & Age Spots, She Applied This Cream For 21 Days' (436,223 | 14.2x).
    Apply: Put a number in the hook and one timeframe per slide (week 1, week 3, week 6, month 3). A scannable ingredient-vs-timeline table is a save-and-send asset — make it one clean slide, not a paragraph.
  6. Credentialed or proxy proof (derm/aesthetician, or 'my mom') outperforms unsourced claims
    Evidence: 'Beginner Retinol tips that will save your skin… from a 40 yo dermatologist' (512,723), 'Rating Retinols as a medical aesthetician that works in derm' (254,903 | 181.9x), 'My mom is 60 ... Everyone thinks she's 40 This is what she uses on her wrinkles' (856,819 | 21x), 'korean anti aging hacks that have people thinking my mom is my sister' (135,527 | 149.4x).
    Apply: Attach the authority to the hook itself: a title ('from a medical aesthetician'), an age ('at 40'), or a living proof subject ('my mom is 60'). Show the mature face on the visual — proof needs a face, not a bottle.
  7. One hero ingredient — retinol — carries the niche; collagen and eye-area are the strong second tier
    Evidence: Retinol appears in at least 14 of the top-30 hooks including 'It's literally just a retinol' (7,563,892 | 142.8x) and 'RETINOL. (most people use it wrong)' (1,317,743 | 20.2x). Collagen/eye hooks: 'High Collagen' (261,137 | 4080.3x), 'WHY IS COLLAGEN IMPORTANT?' (189,499 | 129.8x), 'Rating eye creams for fine lines & wrinkles' (992,823 | 68x), 'Honest Eye Cream Review...' (764,500 | 21x).
    Apply: Anchor every carousel to one named ingredient in the hook (retinol, retinal, peptides, collagen) rather than a vague 'anti-aging routine'. Reserve peptide/collagen content for the eye-area and 'gentler than retinol' framings that the collagen hooks already prove demand for.

Slide structure

  • Slide count: 6 slides (the brief's format). The dataset cannot confirm an optimal count — slide count is 'unknown' for 100% of items — so 6 is chosen as a working standard: 1 hook, 4 payload, 1 soft close.
  • Arc: Proof-reveal arc: (1) hook on the 'before'/problem state, (2) name the one ingredient and the one concern, (3) the mechanism or the mistake most people make, (4) the timeline (week 1 / week 3 / month 3), (5) the visible 'after' or the verdict, (6) the how-to-start line. narrative_arc is 'unknown' for 100% of the corpus, so this arc is inferred from the winning hook types (before_after lift 1.75, comparison and timeline hooks) rather than measured.
  • Text density: Low on slides 1 and 5-6 (under ~10 words, one idea per slide); medium on the timeline/comparison slide, which is the screenshot asset. No panel-word data exists in this dataset (panel words: median n/a overall, n/a in top tier), so treat this as a legibility rule, not a measured pattern.
  • Visual style: Bare-skin close-ups of the actual concern area (under-eyes, smile lines, forehead) shot in consistent light, with ✅/❌ or 🏆/💀 markers on comparison slides and a circled/arrowed detail on the reveal — following 'notice how my smile lines are almost gone' (895,011) which instructs the eye. Face visible, background, foreground and subject age are all 'unknown' (missing data), so no visual attribute is statistically validated.

Content angles

  1. The 3-Week Retinol Diary — A single-subject visual diary of one retinol (or retinal) course: same angle, same light, one slide per checkpoint — day 1, week 1 (the purge/flaking), week 2, week 3 — with an arrow pointing at the smile line or forehead crease that softened. Designer needs a 4-photo consistent set plus a text overlay template with a fixed date stamp corner.
    Emotion: Hopeful impatience — 'so it actually does something, and fast' · Hook: here's my 3 week retinol journey (watch my smile lines)
  2. Best 🏆 vs Worst 💀 for Wrinkles — Verdict carousel judging four common anti-wrinkle habits/products side by side (retinol at night vs retinol under makeup, peptide cream vs heavy occlusive, SPF vs no SPF, collagen serum vs collagen drink). Each slide is a split panel with a trophy on one side and a skull on the other plus one line of reasoning.
    Emotion: Validation and mild fear of doing it wrong · Hook: Best 🏆 vs Worst 💀 for crow's feet
  3. Retinol vs Retinal vs Peptides — A decision-tree comparison for anyone paralysed in the actives aisle: strength, irritation risk, how long to results, and who each is for (sensitive skin, 40+, first-timers). Ends on a 'pick your lane' slide with three labelled routes. Designer needs a clean three-column card that reads at thumbnail size.
    Emotion: Relief from confusion · Hook: Retinol vs retinal vs peptides — which is yours?
  4. How Long Until You See It — The screenshot-bait timeline table: retinol, peptides, collagen cream, vitamin C and SPF each with a realistic 'first visible change' window, then a slide on what to expect in the ugly weeks so people don't quit at week two. One hero table slide plus supporting week-by-week cards.
    Emotion: Reassured patience · Hook: HOW LONG TO SEE RESULTS? retinol, peptides, collagen
  5. My Mom Is 58 — Proxy-proof carousel: photos of a real 55-60 year old, then the four things actually in her routine for wrinkles and neck laxity (retinol night, peptide eye cream, collagen moisturiser, daily SPF) with her exact application order. Mature skin shown unretouched and in close-up — the face is the evidence.
    Emotion: Aspirational trust ('I want to age like her') · Hook: My mom is 58, everyone thinks she's 40

Carousel briefs

  1. 3-week retinol diary
    Six slides tracking one face through a first retinol course at a fixed angle and light: the bare 'before', the week-1 flaking nobody warns you about, week 2 calm, week 3 with an arrow on the softened smile line, then the exact strength and frequency used. The last slide gives the one rule that made it tolerable — pea-size, two nights a week to start.
    Audience: Women 30-42 starting their first retinol who are scared of peeling and quitting in week one · Tone: Honest, unpolished, encouraging, specific · Hook: Me before retinol. Swipe to week 3.
  2. Retinol vs retinal verdict
    A head-to-head that settles the confusion in six swipes: what each molecule actually is, conversion steps and real strength, irritation risk, how fast each shows results, and who should pick which. Final slide is a two-lane 'sensitive skin go here / tolerant skin go here' verdict card.
    Audience: Women 35-50 with reactive or rosacea-prone skin who abandoned retinol once already · Tone: Clear, authoritative, no-hype, practical · Hook: Retinol vs Retinal — which one should you use?
  3. Mom is 58 routine
    Proxy-proof carousel showing a real 58-year-old's face in close-up, then the four products she actually uses on wrinkles, neck and under-eyes, in application order, with the one habit she's kept for 20 years. Ends on what she deliberately does NOT do to her skin.
    Audience: Women 45-60 with mature skin who think it's too late to start actives · Tone: Warm, admiring, credible, unfiltered · Hook: My mom is 58. Everyone thinks she's 40.

Avoid

  • Don't default to tutorial/routine hooks — tutorial_promise is 13% of all posts but only 5% of top tier (lift 0.39, median 114,211 views), the weakest type in the table. Reframe routine content as a diary, verdict or timeline instead.
  • Don't add to the bold-claim pile: bold_claim is 43% of everything posted yet only 32% of top tier (lift 0.74) with the lowest median of the named types (110,649 views). Claims without a visible face, credential or timeline underperform.
  • Skip ingestible / 'eat your actives' framings even though they scored views ('DRINK YOUR RETINOL for clear skin' 8,679,743; 'Whatever you do... DRINK YOUR RETINOL' 4,660,357; 'Eat your retinol >>>' 244,088). They are off-brand-safety for a topical skincare line and imply dietary-health outcomes you can't substantiate.
  • Never use absolute cure language modelled on 'I Reversed My Wrinkles with this 2-Ingredient SECRET' (985,104), 'Japanese people mix these 2 ingredients & never get Wrinkles' (217,827) or '52 Yr Old Mom Has Zero Wrinkles & Age Spots' (436,223) — 'reversed', 'zero wrinkles' and 'never get wrinkles' are unsupportable claims and the mystery-secret framing sits in the low-lift bold_claim bucket anyway.
  • Don't rely on plain question hooks as a differentiator — question is 11% of all and 11% of top tier (lift 0.99, median 144,286). Questions only broke out when they carried a data table ('HOW LONG TO SEE RESULTS?' 2,469,669) or a comparison ('Retinol vs Retinal Which one should you use?' 80,447).
  • Don't build a hard-sell CTA slide: 100% of all posts and 100% of the top tier have no CTA (has CTA: no), so there is zero evidence a sales close belongs here — end on the how-to-start tip and keep the product mention incidental.
  • Don't hide the payoff behind vague slide-1 copy or product-flatlay-only visuals — the highest-lift type is before_after (1.75) and its winners name the exact area ('notice how my smile lines are almost gone', 895,011; 'Tired eyes Before Dark circles...swipe to see what my under eyes area looks like now!', 1,018,053). No visible concern area, no lift.
