SEC 01

The plan

Target customers similar to the ones you already have. Your best clients, Hydraulic Supply, Versa Valves, Aladdin Temp-Rite and ACG, are established American manufacturers and distributors with big technical catalogs and an ERP behind the website. And they all share the trait that matters most:

High retention, great lifetime value. Every one has stayed for years. A client who stays for years is worth many times the first project, which means we can afford to pursue each one carefully. It also means the right goal is not more leads. It is more clients like these.

SEC 02

What other offshore lead agencies typically do

  • They sell you cheap leads by the hundred, priced to look like a bargain.
  • Those leads are bargain hunters themselves: they compare you on price, sign small, and leave early.
  • Your team then burns hours chasing and sitting through meetings that were never going to close.
  • Volume looks great on a monthly report. It does not build a business, and it wastes the time of the people you can least afford to waste.

This plan runs the opposite way: fewer, better-fit meetings with owners of companies that match your best clients. Twenty right meetings a month beats sixty wrong ones.

SEC 03

The positioning, and why it means higher rates

The goal underneath everything: position you as the agency that uniquely understands these companies' problems. ERP systems that hold the truth while the website guesses. Catalogs with 10,000 parts and no way to order them. The fear of upsetting the distributor channel. The counter business that cannot be broken.

Agencies that get compared are priced. Experts who clearly understand the client's world are hired, at higher rates, with less negotiation. Every asset below is built to send one message: we know your business, and we have done this for companies exactly like yours. The four client stories are the proof no competitor can copy.

A 67-year-old distributor doesn't trust an agency's promise. They trust a peer's story.
SEC 04

What we build

Build 1Weeks 1–3

Identify segments and messaging

  • Split the market into exact slices: fluid power distributors, valve makers, foodservice equipment, broad industrial. Each anchored to a client story you can name.
  • Build the target list from Apollo, Explee's AI search (US distributor, big catalog, no online store), and trade association member lists. Every contact verified. The top 500 dream accounts marked for personal treatment.
  • One tested message per slice: the peer story, your ERP knows your stock but your website doesn't, after-hours ordering, the cost of re-typed orders, saw you're hiring for eCommerce, the project rescue.
Build 2Weeks 2–4

Custom landing pages

  • One page per segment and message. A fluid power distributor clicking an ad lands on a page about a fluid power distributor, not a generic agency page.
  • Each page: the named client story with numbers, then a free Catalog and ERP eCommerce Assessment as the single call to action. A specific, bounded first step an owner will actually take.
  • An ROI calculator for the sales calls: order volume and re-typing costs in, payback on the project out.
Build 3Weeks 3–8 →

Ad campaigns with InMail and Facebook ads

  • InMail ($2k/month): hand-written LinkedIn messages to the top 500 owners, each one built on real research about their company. The premium channel for the premium accounts.
  • Facebook ads ($3–5k/month): client-story video and carousel creative. Retargeting everyone who visits the site or engages on LinkedIn, plus audiences built from the target list. Every ad names a real client, speaks to one slice, and points at its matching landing page.
  • Email outreach underneath: 15–20k emails a month through Explee (about 3¢ each) carrying the same messages to the rest of the market. Interested replies get a human answer within the hour and a meeting booked by phone, never a form.
  • Campaigns are judged on one number: cost per signed client. Not clicks, not opens.
Build 4Weeks 2–6 →

7 to 10 LinkedIn leadership posts

  • Written for the founder's profile, each one showing real knowledge of the space: teardowns (we looked at 50 distributor websites, 41 can't show live stock), ERP explainers, client-story carousels, channel-conflict takes.
  • These posts are what owners find when they check you out after an InMail or an email. They do the convincing silently. The more a prospect has read before a call, the more likely they show up and buy.
  • The best-performing posts become the ad creative. Content and campaigns feed each other.

SEC 08

Five questions before we start

None of these block week 1. Every answer makes the plan sharper.

  1. What is a client worth to you, first project and over the years?
  2. Can we name your four clients (logos, quotes) in emails and ads?
  3. Which industries do you want more of, and any you'd turn down?
  4. Who takes the meetings, how many can they handle, and who owns follow-up?
  5. Of the first meetings you've had historically, how many signed?
