# Lead Generation Plan: Old Line, New Revenue

**Client:** B2B eCommerce agency for established American manufacturers and distributors
**Prepared:** August 30, 2026 · Rev 8
**Channels:** InMail · Facebook Ads · Email (Explee) · LinkedIn
**Investment:** $5k–7k/month + media
**Sources:** the four client case studies · live Apollo market data (Aug 30, 2026) · Daniel Fazio's acquisition framework

---

## 1. The plan

**Target customers similar to the ones you already have.** Your best clients, Hydraulic Supply, Versa Valves, Aladdin Temp-Rite, ACG, are established American manufacturers and distributors with big technical catalogs and an ERP behind the website. And they share the trait that matters most:

**High retention, great lifetime value.** Every one has stayed for years. A client who stays for years is worth many times the first project, so the right goal is not "more leads," it is *more clients like these*.

The market of look-alikes, counted live in Apollo (US companies, strict decision-maker titles):

| Industry | Company size | Buyers found |
|---|---|---|
| Fluid power · hydraulics · hose and fittings | 21–1,000 people | **2,321** |
| Valves and flow control | 21–1,000 people | **5,994** |
| Foodservice equipment | 21–1,000 people | **2,328** |
| All industrial manufacturing and distribution | 51–1,000 people | **31,774** |

Only **145** people in that entire market hold an eCommerce job title. **The person to reach is the owner or president**, and everything in this plan is written for them: US manufacturer or distributor, 50–1,000 employees, ~$20M–$500M revenue, privately or family owned, big catalog, ERP behind the site.

## 2. What other offshore lead agencies typically do

- They sell you cheap leads by the hundred, priced to look like a bargain.
- Those leads are bargain hunters themselves: they compare you on price, sign small, and leave early.
- Your team then burns hours chasing and sitting through meetings that were never going to close.
- Volume looks great on a monthly report. It does not build a business, and it wastes the time of the people you can least afford to waste.

This plan runs the opposite way: fewer, better-fit meetings with owners of companies that match your best clients. Twenty right meetings a month beats sixty wrong ones.

## 3. The positioning, and why it means higher rates

The goal underneath everything: **position you as the agency that uniquely understands these companies' problems.** ERPs that hold the truth while the website guesses. Catalogs with 10,000 parts and no way to order them. The fear of upsetting the distributor channel. The counter business that cannot break.

Agencies that get compared are priced. Experts who clearly understand the client's world are hired, at higher rates, with less negotiation. Every asset below sends one message: *we know your business, and we have done this for companies exactly like yours.* The four client stories are proof no competitor can copy.

> **A seasoned distributor doesn't trust an agency's promise. They trust a peer's story.**

## 4. What we build

### Build 1: Identify segments and messaging (weeks 1–3)
- Split the market into exact slices, fluid power distribution, valves/flow control, foodservice equipment, broad industrial, each anchored to a client story you can name.
- Target list from Apollo + Explee AI Search ("US distributor, big catalog, no online store") + trade association member lists (NAHAD, FPDA, PTDA, NAFEM, VMA). Every contact verified; top 500 dream accounts marked for personal treatment.
- One tested message per slice: peer story · "your ERP knows your stock, your website doesn't" · after-hours ordering · cost of re-typed orders · hiring-for-eCommerce trigger · project rescue.

### Build 2: Custom landing pages (weeks 2–4)
- One page per segment and message, congruent with the ad or email that brought the visitor.
- Each page: the named client story with numbers → a free **Catalog and ERP eCommerce Assessment** as the single call to action.
- ROI calculator for sales calls: order volume and re-typing costs in, project payback out.

### Build 3: Ad campaigns: InMail and Facebook ads (weeks 3–8, ongoing)
- **InMail ($2k/mo):** hand-written LinkedIn messages to the top 500 owners, built on real account research.
- **Facebook ads ($3–5k/mo):** client-story video and carousel creative, retargeting site visitors and LinkedIn engagers, plus audiences built from the target list. Every ad names a real client, speaks to one slice, and points at its matching landing page.
- **Email outreach underneath:** 15–20k emails/month through Explee AutoGTM (~$0.03 each) carrying the same messages to the rest of the market. Interested replies get a human answer within the hour and a meeting booked by phone, never a form.
- Campaigns judged on one number: cost per signed client.

### Build 4: 7–10 LinkedIn leadership posts (weeks 2–6, ongoing)
- Written for the founder's profile, each showing real knowledge of the space: teardowns ("we looked at 50 distributor websites, 41 can't show live stock"), ERP explainers, client-story carousels, channel-conflict takes.
- These posts are what owners find when they check the agency out after an InMail or email, they convince silently; pre-call content consumption is the #1 predictor of show and close.
- Best-performing posts become the ad creative, content and campaigns feed each other.

## 5. Working backwards to three or four clients a month

| Step | Rate | Needed |
|---|---|---|
| New clients / month | goal | **3–4** |
| Proposals that close | 1 in 4 | 12–16 proposals |
| Meetings that lead to a proposal | 4 in 10 | 30–40 meetings held |
| Booked meetings that show | 8 in 10 | 38–50 booked |
| From email outreach | 15–20k emails/mo | 15–22 |
| From InMail, Facebook ads and timing triggers | balance | 23–28 |

Email carries part of it; the bigger Facebook ad budget and InMail carry the rest to reach three to four. Judge on cost per meeting and cost per client only.

## 6. When the results show up

- **Weeks 5 to 6:** first meetings booked from outreach.
- **Month 2:** 15 to 25 good meetings a month.
- **Month 3:** 38 to 50 good meetings a month, steady.
- **Months 4 to 6:** three to four clients signed a month. The $7k plan, with our team running the ads, gets here faster.

## Appendix: operating notes

- Explee pre-launch checks (pass/fail): whose accounts send, do we control them, replies to our inbox, full export of leads/scripts/replies, human approval of all copy.
- Email quality bar: <2% bounce · plain text · one idea per email · offer stated outright · a named peer client in every send · no links in email 1.
- Judging windows: a message gets a verdict only after 3–5k sends and six weeks of real effort; read raw replies before any pivot. Expect wide week-to-week swings at this meeting volume, don't fix statistical noise with strategy changes.
- Speed to lead on inbound: text in 2–3 min, call in 5–7; abandoned applications to a same-day priority queue; keep booking calendars under ~70% full.
- Weekly nurture email to the owned list + quarterly "State of Distributor eCommerce" report, most revenue comes from prospects who read along 6–12 months before their trigger event.
